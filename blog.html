#50 papers challenge

A lightweight archive of notes on papers I found interesting in AI for Earth observation.

Original idea. My research New Year resolution was to read more papers and post a few lines on interesting — and possibly less widespread — contributions addressing AI4EO challenges. This page preserves that informal spirit rather than turning the notes into formal reviews.

#41

Beyond Grid Data

A useful entry point into graph neural networks for Earth observation, especially for irregular, heterogeneous and multi-source data such as point clouds.

↑ Modeling non-Euclidean spatial structures.
↓ Domain-specific graph construction strategies remain an open question.

#40

PANGAEA

Are geospatial foundation models really impactful? PANGAEA was built to make that question measurable through a diverse benchmark spanning applications, geography, tasks, modalities and temporality.

What we wanted: a robust evaluation protocol; domain generalisation; comparisons to supervised baselines; and low-label regimes.
What became clear: GFMs do not automatically dominate well-tuned supervised baselines, and multi-temporal data remain under-explored.

#39

Contrasting local and global SatML: tree canopy height in African savannas

An interesting study of the trade-off between local and global machine-learning models for geospatial tasks, using tree-canopy-height mapping as a case study.

↑ Strong research questions around local versus global models.
↓ I would like to see the same questions pushed toward generalist geospatial foundation models.

#38

Specialized foundation models struggle to beat supervised baselines

A useful reminder that massive pretraining does not automatically imply better downstream performance. In several specialised domains, carefully tuned supervised baselines remain highly competitive.

↑ Very relevant challenge to the “foundation model by default” assumption.
↓ Classification-only evaluations can limit conclusions about real-world EO usefulness.

#37

ALISE

ALISE learns aligned representations from irregular and unaligned satellite image time series, producing fixed-size representations while preserving spatial resolution.

↑ Great handling of sparse temporal observations and labels.
↓ Interesting to extend the study across broader geographical and sensor domains.

Paper ↗

#36

TaxaBind

A multimodal framework aligning ecological data modalities including ground images, location, satellite images, text, audio and environmental features.

↑ Robust cross-modal and zero-shot capabilities.
↓ What happens when we want to introduce genuinely new downstream tasks?
